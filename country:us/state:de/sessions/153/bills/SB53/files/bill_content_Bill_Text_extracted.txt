WHEREAS, the Farm to Community Program was launched in 2023 using funds from the Local Food Purchase Assistance (LFPA) program established by the United States Department of Agriculture (USDA); and WHEREAS, the Delaware Farm and Food Council (Council), formerly known as the Delaware Council on Farm and Food Policy, created the Program to provide additional market outlets for Delaware and regional producers, as well as to provide fresh and nutritious local food products to underserved communities throughout Delaware; and WHEREAS, through the Farm to Community Program, community outlets like food pantries, community-based organizations, faith-based institutions, and senior centers use funds to procure a variety of fresh, seasonal farm products from local producers and distribute these food products to Delaware communities through food closets, congregate meal sites, community centers, community refrigerators, shelters, and home deliveries, and as part of Food is Medicine initiative and WHEREAS, after 2 1/2 growing seasons, the Farm to Community Program has grown from 2 community outlets and a few farmers to 9 community outlets, 31 farmers, and 105 food distribution sites in New Castle, Kent, and Sussex counties, with more than $1,000,000 worth of local food products distributed to Delaware communities; and WHEREAS, the tangible outcomes of the Farm to Community Program show that this model is effective to stimulate local food production, procurement, aggregation, and transporting, and to expand the distribution capacity for providing healthy, seasonal food options to Delawareans in urban and rural communities, including the most vulnerable populations; and WHEREAS, individuals, communities, and governments are facing increasing challenges and disruptions to local food systems; and WHEREAS, strengthening local supply chains means being better prepared for emergencies and better equipped to ensure that Delawareans can always access food even when systems shift; and WHEREAS, after the USDA abruptly canceled its âLFPA 2025â program, eliminating funding to all participating states and jeopardizing the security and sustainability of the Farm to Community Program; and WHEREAS, codifying the Farm to Community Program ensures continuity and the opportunity for the State to build direct relationships between farms and communities, promote stability and strengthen the local food system infrastructure, facilitate public-private partnerships, and direct resources to address food gaps and optimize local food supply chains.
